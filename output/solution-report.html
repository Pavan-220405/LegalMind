SOLUTION REPORT | LEGALMIND AI

Grounded legal help and debate practice

A two-workflow legal assistant for everyday disputes in India
Problem and choice. People facing routine disputes such as unreturned rental deposits, defective purchases, or unpaid wages need a clear first step but may lack easy access to legal guidance. We chose a focused assistant that structures the issue, retrieves relevant legal excerpts, explains options, and supports drafting or debate practice. A domain-bounded, evidence-first workflow is more useful here than an unrestricted legal chatbot, while still requiring a human lawyer for consequential decisions.

Scope and assumptions

Supported areas. Rent and tenancy, consumer protection, and wages. A general fallback handles other topics without presenting them as verified domain advice.
Context. India is the default country context. State, forum, dates, contract terms, law commencement, and facts can change the result. Uploaded PDFs are context, not automatically authoritative law.
Role and access. Streamlit text interface with PDF upload and a separate Debate Practice mode. The product provides information and drafting practice, not legal representation; it is not yet multilingual or voice-enabled.
Safety boundary. The app checks for certain urgent or crisis signals before model routing. Unclear domain intake can ask a follow-up rather than force an answer. High-risk or time-sensitive matters need qualified local review.

Evidence pipeline

PDF ingestion extracts selectable text and uses OCR where needed, then conservatively cleans and structure-chunks content. Persistent Chroma collections are separated by legal domain. Chunk IDs are deterministic; source, page, section, and other metadata are retained so changed sources can be updated and returned passages traced.

Multi-query retrieval. The original question plus three optional LLM rewrites broadens recall when everyday wording differs from statutory wording. Alternatives are checked for protected details such as section numbers, years, dates, amounts, and durations. Results merge by stable chunk ID, preventing repeats.
Hybrid search. BGE-M3 embeddings with Chroma find semantically similar passages; BM25 finds exact terms and statutory labels. LangChain EnsembleRetriever fuses ranked lists with reciprocal-rank fusion, default weights 0.5/0.5, to produce up to 20 candidates inside the selected domain.
Reranking. A Voyage cross-encoder ranks candidates against the original query and returns up to six. A Cohere reranker is tried if Voyage responds with a rate limit (HTTP 429); other errors surface. This improves relevance ordering but cannot establish that a passage is current or legally applicable.
Grounding contract. Provenance follows each passage into the response and citations point to retrieved source IDs. If no usable excerpts are found, the system should abstain from domain-specific legal claims. Matching a citation ID alone does not prove that a passage entails the attached claim.
Question → safety check → supervisor → domain retrieval → analysis / draft → compliance and section checks → response

WORKFLOWS | OBSERVABILITY | VALIDATION

Two agents for two user goals

Normal legal-help agent

Implemented as a LangGraph StateGraph, the normal workflow records the turn, runs a local safety check, and asks a structured supervisor to classify the issue. The graph routes to rent, consumer, or wages retrieval agents, requests clarification when routing confidence is low, and uses a cautious general fallback outside those domains. Domain nodes return structured analysis with retrieved sources. Drafting then shapes a notice or other response, followed by checks for citation IDs, urgency, limitation warnings, and unsupported statutory section numbers before the conversation node replies.

Debater agent

A separate LangGraph first clarifies the issue, jurisdiction, user position, and readiness to practise. After confirmation, it routes to the matching rent, consumer, or wages vertical, retrieves excerpts, and argues the opposing side with source labels. Conversation history supports turn-by-turn rebuttal; a coaching request switches to feedback on strengths, gaps, and a concrete improvement. If retrieval is empty, it declines to invent a law-based argument. It is practice, not adjudication or legal advice.

Framework, state, and LangSmith

The stack is Python, LangChain for model and retriever integrations, LangGraph for explicit stateful routing, Chroma for persistent vectors, BM25 for lexical search, and Streamlit for the interface. Groq or Google models can be configured; workflow dependencies are injectable for controlled evaluation. In the UI, both graphs use in-memory checkpoints. The normal graph also supports PostgreSQL checkpointing for durable thread history; debate persistence is currently in-memory by default.

Optional LangSmith tracing is wired into both streamed workflows. Set LANGSMITH_TRACING=true, an API key, and optionally endpoint/project settings to inspect graph runs, node paths, latency, and errors. Tracing is observability, not a quality verdict. Because inputs may include sensitive legal narratives or uploaded-document text, deployment should set data retention and access controls deliberately; the report does not assume traces are redacted.

How we evaluate it and scale it

Retrieval: documented cases cover exact statutory terms and semantic questions across each domain. Compare dense, BM25, and hybrid modes; toggle query expansion and reranking; inspect collection isolation, duplicate IDs, provenance, and score metadata.

Debate: a tenant deposit case checks clarification before retrieval, correct domain selection, excerpt-supported opposition, continuity across turns, coaching, and abstention when sources are missing.

Quality: use expert-reviewed prompts and expected source sets. Review citation entailment, jurisdiction/date handling, factual fidelity in drafts, safe abstentions, and urgent-case routing. Track retrieval hit quality, unsupported claims, abstentions, latency, and provider cost. Add regression and adversarial cases before expanding coverage.

Scale: ingest reviewed, versioned central and state sources with effective dates; add jurisdiction-aware routing and expert evaluation before enabling each new topic. Keep critical safety, scope, and citation checks deterministic where possible. Limit sensitive data access and retention, provide human escalation, and monitor service capacity and costs.

Known limits and failure modes. This is an early prototype without independent legal validation. OCR may corrupt text; source packs may be incomplete or stale; query rewrites can drift; a reranker can misorder passages; and a fluent model can still overstate what the evidence says. Current compliance checks validate citation IDs, flag urgency and limitation uncertainty, and correct unsupported section numbers, but do not perform full claim-entailment or jurisdiction audits. Consumer limitation logic is only a date-based heuristic; rent and wage deadlines are left uncertain. Human review is needed before filing or sending a legal notice.

Implementation status: the repository includes manual retrieval and debate scenarios. Those scenarios are evaluation plans, not evidence of measured accuracy or successful independent legal review. For a full component map, see the architecture diagram in the README.
